OmniAct3D: Leveraging Foundation Geometry and Evidence-Grounded
Reasoning for Panoramic 3D Detection
Runtong Wu1,∗, Fei Teng1,∗, Di Wen2, Guoqiang Zhao1, Kunyu Peng2, and Kailun Yang1,†
Abstract— Accurate 3D detection is essential for mobile
embodied agents, while Vision Foundation Models (VFMs)
offer transferable visual and geometric priors. Yet existing
VFM-based 3D detectors rely on narrow-view monocular im-
ages or discrete perspective views, limiting coherent surround
perception; equirectangular projection (ERP) instead encodes
a continuous 360◦scene in a single image. Direct transfer
remains difficult because ERP organizes geometry and visual
information differently, making object-relevant cues hard to
model, localize, and preserve. We propose OMNIACT3D, a
framework that adapts perspective-trained VFM detectors to
ERP while preserving transferable VFM priors. To resolve
geometric mismatch, the ERP-Ray Geometry Adapter (ERGA-
RAY) models spherical viewing rays and periodic spatial struc-
ture. To localize evidence in scene-wide context, the Visual-
Action Reasoning Chain (VARC) grounds each hypothesis in
relevant panoramic evidence and converts it into a structured
geometric action. To recover local cues lost under fixed token
budgets, the Appearance-Guided Heading Expert (AGHE) re-
encodes object regions at higher resolution for heading estima-
tion. Experiments show that OMNIACT3D improves over the
previous best 3D detector by 2.96 NDS points on Spheriverse
and over the unadapted VFM baseline by 24.87 mAP points on
PanoMMOcc. With target-specific geometry adaptation, VARC
retains 95–98% of the same-configuration mAP, indicating
reusable object-level 3D reasoning across sensing configura-
tions. The source code will be made publicly available at
https://github.com/FeiT-FeiTeng/OmniAct3D.

embodied agents to understand and interact with the physical
world. From autonomous vehicles to mobile robots, agents
must estimate the 3D locations, dimensions, and orientations
of surrounding objects to support navigation, motion plan-
ning, and embodied decision-making [1], [2].

ferable visual and geometric priors for object-level 3D per-
ception. Despite this progress, existing methods primarily
follow two perspective-image paradigms. As shown in Fig. 1,
monocular methods [3], [7] infer 3D boxes from a single

However, directly transferring perspective-trained VFM-
based 3D detectors to ERP observations remains challenging
because ERP organizes geometry and visual information
differently from perspective imagery. This representation
mismatch makes object-relevant cues difficult to model,
localize, and preserve, giving rise to three challenges. First,
the geometric priors of VFMs are learned predominantly
from locally planar and non-periodic perspective imagery,
whereas ERP exhibits spherical viewing directions, latitude-
dependent distortion, and horizontal periodicity. Reusing
these priors without adaptation therefore produces geometric
representations misaligned with ERP structure, particularly
at high latitudes and across the horizontal boundary. Second,
although a single ERP image captures scene-wide context,
the evidence relevant to each object occupies only a small
portion of the panorama. Directly decoding 3D hypotheses
from global features makes it difficult to localize and as-

sociate the supporting evidence for each object, especially
when objects are small or occluded. Third, encoding an
ultra-wide panorama under a fixed token budget compresses
individual objects and discards fine-grained local appearance
cues. The loss of features such as front–rear asymmetry
impairs directed heading estimation.
To address these challenges, we propose OMNIACT3D,
a framework that adapts perspective-trained VFM detectors
to ERP observations while preserving transferable VFM
priors. To resolve the geometric mismatch, we introduce the
ERP-Ray Geometry Adapter (ERGA-RAY), which models
spherical viewing rays and ERP-aware spatial interactions
to align pretrained foundation geometry with ERP-specific
spherical and periodic structure. To localize object-relevant
evidence in scene-wide context, we introduce the Visual-
Action Reasoning Chain (VARC), which grounds each 3D
hypothesis in relevant panoramic evidence and converts that
evidence into a structured geometric action for object-level
3D reasoning. To recover local cues lost under a fixed token
budget, we introduce the AGHE, which re-encodes object
regions at higher resolution for directed heading estimation.
We evaluate OMNIACT3D on Spheriverse [12] and
PanoMMOcc [13]. On Spheriverse, OMNIACT3D achieves
0.1887/0.1825 mAP/NDS, improving the previous best NDS
by 2.96 points. On PanoMMOcc, it achieves 0.5897/0.4032
mAP/NDS and improves the unadapted VFM baseline by
24.87 mAP points. Moreover, with target-specific geometry
adaptation via ERGA-RAY, VARC retains 95% and 98% of
same-configuration mAP on Spheriverse and PanoMMOcc,
respectively, indicating reusable object-level 3D reasoning
across sensing configurations.
In summary, our contributions are as follows:
• We propose OMNIACT3D, a framework that adapts
perspective-trained VFM detectors to ERP observations
while preserving transferable VFM priors.
• We introduce ERGA-RAY to align foundation geometry
with ERP spherical and periodic structure.
• We introduce VARC to ground 3D hypotheses in rele-
vant panoramic evidence and convert that evidence into
a structured geometric action, and AGHE to recover
fine-grained local cues for directed heading estimation.
• Experiments show that OMNIACT3D improves NDS
by 2.96 points over the previous best detector on
Spheriverse and mAP by 24.87 points over the un-
adapted VFM baseline on PanoMMOcc; with target-
specific geometry adaptation, VARC retains 95–98% of
same-configuration mAP across sensing configurations.
II. RELATED WORK
A. Vision-Based 3D Object Detection
Vision-based 3D object detection estimates object loca-
tions, dimensions, and orientations from images. Monocular
methods infer 3D states from a single perspective view using
geometric constraints, depth estimation, or object-centric rep-
resentations [14], [15], [16]. Multi-view methods aggregate
complementary observations through geometric projection,
query-based aggregation, or BEV representations [17], [18],
[19], while temporal approaches further exploit cross-frame
information [20]. These methods are developed primarily for
perspective imagery. Monocular systems have limited fields
of view, whereas surround-view systems rely on multiple
discrete camera views and cross-view integration. In contrast,
our work studies object-level 3D detection directly from a
panoramic representation for continuous 360◦perception.
B. Omnidirectional Vision and ERP Perception
Equirectangular projection (ERP) represents a spherical
field of view within a single image and naturally provides
spatially continuous horizontal 360◦coverage. ERP imagery
has been studied for panoramic depth estimation, scene
understanding, 3D detection, and omnidirectional occupancy
prediction [21], [22], [23], [24]. However, ERP geometry dif-
fers fundamentally from perspective imaging due to latitude-
dependent distortion, horizontal periodicity, and spherical
viewing rays. Prior work addresses these properties through
spherical convolutions and distortion sampling [25], [26],
projection fusion [27], and panoramic attention [28]. These
works highlight the need for ERP-specific geometric model-
ing for coherent panoramic perception.
C. Foundation Models for Panoramic 3D Perception
Large-scale VFMs provide transferable priors for se-
mantic and geometric perception. Visual models such as
DINO [29], [30] and SAM [31], [32] learn reusable visual
representations, while geometry foundation models such as
UniDepth [33], MoGe [34], and VGGT [4] provide trans-
ferable priors for depth and 3D structure. We use the term
VFM-based 3D detector for a 3D detector built on such
pretrained models [6], [35], [36], [37]. Related work also
develops camera-general geometry [38], [39], [40], [41],
[42], [43], [44], [45]. Despite their transferability, adapting
VFM-based 3D detection from perspective imagery to ERP
remains challenging. We address this perspective-to-ERP
adaptation through ERP geometry alignment and evidence-
grounded object-level 3D reasoning.

E. Training Objectives
ERGA-RAY is supervised by a multi-target loss:
LERGA = Ltarget + λoriLori + λpreLpre
+ λconfLconf + λresLres.
(17)
Here, Ltarget jointly regresses the seam-aware image-center
residual, log-range log R, and log-dimensions. The remain-
ing terms preserve orientation, pretrained regression, and
confidence predictions, while constraining the magnitude of
the learned adaptation residual. VARC is supervised by the
following objective:
LVARC = Lb + λidLid + λgLg + λrLr.
(18)
Here, Lb supervises the resulting spherical box state, Lid
encourages identity actions for already accurate hypotheses,
and Lg supervises action-gate activation. Lr denotes the
radial loss, combining direction classification, action ranking,
and action-magnitude regression. The AGHE is supervised
on the sine-cosine encoding of the periodic observation-
relative heading in Eq. (16) using a cosine loss.
IV. EXPERIMENTS
We evaluate OMNIACT3D on two panoramic 3D detec-
tion benchmarks. We then analyze the effects of ERGA-
RAY, VARC, and AGHE, and test whether VARC transfers
between different sensing configurations.
A. Experimental Setup
Datasets and metrics. We use Spheriverse [12] and PanoM-
MOcc [13], which were originally developed for 3D oc-
cupancy prediction. Both datasets provide LiDAR obser-
vations, 3D bounding boxes, and camera calibration. We
transform the boxes from the LiDAR coordinate system to
the panoramic camera coordinate system to construct the two
ERP 3D detection benchmarks. The datasets differ in camera
height, vertical field of view, and object-distance distribution.
Following the nuScenes-style protocol [54], we report mAP,
NDS, mATE, mASE, and mAOE as evaluation metrics. The
two benchmarks do not provide the attribute and velocity
annotations required for mAAE and mAVE. We therefore
report a three-TP variant of NDS.
Baselines and evaluation protocol. We compare against
representative 3D detectors spanning Cartesian-grid BEV
architectures [18], [20], [47], geometry-oriented BEV archi-
tectures [50], [51], polar-coordinate BEV architectures [48],
sparse query-based architectures [17], [19], configuration-
aware architectures [49], monocular detectors [52], and
VFM-based detectors [36], [37], [53]. In Table I, WildDet3D
uses its original square preprocessing scheme, while 3D-
MOOD and DetAny3D decompose each panorama into
perspective views. The remaining methods operate directly
on the full rectangular equirectangular projection (ERP)
panorama. All ablations use the same ERP protocol. Unless
otherwise noted, all methods are evaluated on both bench-
marks with only minimal dataset-specific adaptations.
Implementation details. All experiments are conducted on
four NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3090 GPUs. The VFM-based
3D detector remains frozen throughout training, and all
input images are resized to 2480 × 512 pixels. We optimize
ERGA-RAY, VARC, and AGHE separately, updating one
component at a time while keeping the others fixed. ERGA-
RAY and VARC together introduce approximately 17.07M
trainable parameters, corresponding to only a 0.15% increase
in the total parameter count, while incurring a measured
inference-latency overhead of 3.3%–5.0%. For VARC trans-
fer, we use a target-specific ERGA-Ray module.

V. ANALYSIS AND ABLATION STUDIES
We analyze equirectangular projection (ERP) geome-
try adaptation, hypothesis-evidence-action reasoning, fine-
grained heading recovery, and reasoning reuse.
A. Component Ablation Analysis
Table II reports the component ablation. Adding VARC to
ERGA-RAY improves mAP by 6.96 points on Spheriverse
(0.1887 vs. 0.1191) and by 15.44 points on PanoMMOcc
(0.5897 vs. 0.4353). Adding AGHE further reduces mAOE
while leaving mAP unchanged. Thus, VARC mainly im-
proves object localization, whereas AGHE improves heading
estimation in full OMNIACT3D.
B. ERP Geometry Adaptation
ERP-specific geometry modeling. To investigate whether
existing adaptation methods are sufficient for ERP 3D
detection, Table III compares several methods using the
same LingBot backbone [46]. No Adapt. changes only the
projection to ERP and directly uses the original LingBot
features. MLP and AdaptFormer [55] apply feature adap-
tation, while Circular Conv [56], Ray-FiLM [57], and the
PanoSwin-based [28] model horizontal periodicity, spherical
viewing rays, and panoramic spatial interactions, respec-
tively. ERGA-RAY combines spherical ray conditioning with
periodic spatial modeling. Overall, ERGA-RAY improves
mAP/NDS over the strongest alternatives by 21.5%/9.1%
on Spheriverse and by 16.7%/11.1% on PanoMMOcc.
Geometry backbone comparison. To test whether ERGA-
RAY is effective with different geometry backbones, Table III
also evaluates UniK3D [39], DA2 [40], and DAP [41].
UniK3D is designed for general camera models, while DA2
and DAP are designed for panoramic images. Although
DAP with ERGA-RAY has lower mAP than LingBot with
ERGA-RAY, adding ERGA-RAY still improves DAP by
44.4% on Spheriverse (0.0986 vs. 0.0683) and by 13.1% on
PanoMMOcc (0.2097 vs. 0.1854). This shows that ERGA-
RAY is also effective with a different backbone.

D. Fine-Grained Heading Recovery
We evaluate whether object-centric re-encoding recovers
local appearance cues weakened by global ERP encoding.
Table II shows that the AGHE leaves mAP unchanged
while reducing mAOE from 1.5585 to 0.8455 on Spheriverse
and from 2.0781 to 0.7902 on PanoMMOcc, increasing
NDS to 0.1825 and 0.4032. Thus, AGHE improves heading
estimation without changing object localization.

E. Cross-Configuration Reasoning Transfer
To investigate whether VARC remains reusable across
sensing configurations, we transfer it between the two
datasets without updating its parameters, using the target-
specific ERGA-RAY. As shown in Table VII, the transferred
VARC retains 95% and 98% of same-configuration mAP
on Spheriverse and PanoMMOcc, respectively (0.1800 vs.
0.1887 and 0.5803 vs. 0.5897). This shows that object-level
reasoning is preserved after transfer.
VI. CONCLUSION
We present OMNIACT3D for perspective-to-ERP adapta-
tion of VFM-based 3D detection. The framework addresses
the three requirements of this central challenge: ERGA-RAY
aligns pretrained foundation geometry with ERP-specific
structure, VARC establishes an explicit hypothesis-evidence-
action reasoning chain for evidence-grounded object-level
3D reasoning, and an AGHE preserves local cues for di-
rected heading estimation. OMNIACT3D achieves the best
mAP and NDS on two ERP benchmarks with heteroge-
neous sensing configurations. VARC also retains strong
performance across configurations when paired with target-
specific ERGA-Ray. These results highlight the effectiveness
of OMNIACT3D for mobile embodied agents. Future work
will explore extending evidence-grounded object-level 3D
reasoning to temporal panoramic observations and more
complex embodied perception tasks.
